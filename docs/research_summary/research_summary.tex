\begin{abstract}
\noindent
I ask whether a small causal transformer, trained from scratch only to predict the next
symbol of sequences emitted by a hidden random walk on a world of known topology, develops
internal structure that reflects that topology, where such structure comes from, and whether
the model uses it. The pilot compares a $12\times12$ cylinder with a $12\times12$ square under
matched dynamics and emissions, with an exact Bayesian oracle, matched emission controls, and
representation baselines, and decides one primary hypothesis by a rule frozen before any
model was trained. Under the smooth emission, training produces a persistent-$H_1$ signal in
the final residual stream that separates the cylinder from the square in 24 of 24 panels
across three seeds (median contrast 0.158--0.176 against a noise envelope of 0.084--0.097),
and its leading $H_1$ class aligns with the world's periodic coordinate (agreement 0.88--0.89,
exact-belief ceiling 0.92). The activation geometry tracks the 32-symbol Bayesian belief more
than the last-symbol posterior. A timing hypothesis was rejected, the matched permuted-emission
control turned out to be one-sided, the last-symbol embedding alone carries 0.47--0.89 of the
contrast, linear probes recover no more belief than the exact next-symbol law does, and a
clean-rerun gate failed on one missing historical record. More than a dozen follow-up
studies, almost all preregistered, then went after the origin of the circular structure, its causal use, and the necessity
of topological distinctions for longer prediction horizons. Most stopped at preregistered
feasibility gates (INCONCLUSIVE) or returned scoped negatives; positive results are exact
(oracle) facts about designed processes: an orientability distinction needed only two steps
ahead, component identity that requires long history, and a process whose predictions depend on
the order of loop crossings. In the most direct origin test,
the covariance of the learned chart phase with the part of the belief that the last four
observations do not determine was excluded from beyond a $\pm0.01$ margin in all three models;
the registered margin, however, lies above what the oracle's own phase achieves. A
crossing-order study trains twelve models on a non-abelian ($S_3$) transport process to test
whether trained transformers use the order of seam crossings beyond every commutative summary;
all validity gates passed and the preregistered outcome is INCONCLUSIVE. In every seed
the trained models carry a small but clearly non-zero order-dependent component on natural
histories (mean $\tilde\beta=0.061$, lower bound $0.0595$; commutative controls and
initialisations near zero), an order of magnitude below the registered effect size of $0.25$,
and they do not beat the bar on the designed witness histories; one commutative control's
response to a designed placebo prevents the registered ``order not detected'' verdict.
The latest study gives the first causal-use result for a trained model. After the final
LayerNorm the readout is exactly linear, so the model's own response to natural counterfactual
histories (the $x$-history rotated by two cells, the last symbol kept) can be attributed
exactly to its circle code. Interchanging the six coordinates of the registered,
nuisance-excluding circle code carries 85\% of the model's signed oracle-aligned response
($\hat\psi=0.851$--$0.856$, lower bounds far above the preregistered 0.50) in all three pilot
models: the readout uses this code (USED). The same holds for the layer-2 code transmitted
through the remaining computation and for a code built from the oracle's phase. The claim is
scoped to the registered linear code; it does not exclude other encodings of the same
information. A follow-up on fresh histories shows that the code is the main carrier but not
the only one (everything outside it carries 14--15\% of the response, in all three models), and
that the method can say ``not used'': on decoy models whose readout ignores the code exactly,
while the code stays in the activations, it returned NOT\_USED in all three. A check with no
training found that one commutative control's response to the designed crossing-order placebo
replicates on fresh histories but matches neither order use nor a simple miscount, so its cause
remains unexplained. An oracle-only sweep of 225 worlds found one, the same cylinder with wider
emissions, in which the oracle's own beyond-four-symbol signal is 4.7 times the registered
margin; a small training pilot in that world is under way.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Large language models are trained to predict the next token, yet they are often described as
carrying ``world models''. Whether, and in what sense, next-token training produces
representations that mirror the latent structure of the process generating the data is an
empirical question that is hard to answer on natural text, because the latent structure is
unknown. Synthetic processes whose latent state, dynamics and exact Bayesian predictor are
fully known make the question tractable. Prior work established that transformers trained on
hidden Markov models (HMMs) linearly represent the geometry of the Bayesian belief state in
their residual stream \citep{shai2024belief,piotrowski2025constrained}, and that sequence models
trained on games or walks can carry recoverable world state
\citep{li2023othello,vafa2024worldmodel,brenner2026grid}. Topology---global invariants such as
the number of independent loops---had not been controlled in this setting.

The \textbf{topo-pilot} project trains a small causal transformer from scratch on symbols
emitted by a lazy random walk on one of two worlds with the same number of states and the same
local rule: a \emph{cylinder} (one independent loop, $\beta_1=1$) and a \emph{square}
($\beta_1=0$). It asks three nested questions, fixed in the binding specification
\fpath{docs/RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md} \S1 and later in the owner's research charter
(\fpath{agent-handoff/NEXT_RESEARCH_AND_WORKFLOW_EN.md}):
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*}]
\item \textbf{Existence.} Does the context-dependent representation acquire structure that
reflects the latent topology?
\item \textbf{Origin.} If so, does that structure come from the geometry of the observations,
from beliefs accumulated over the history, or only from what is needed to predict the next
token? (Charter priority~1: how much of the circular structure is explained by the last
symbol or a short context, and how much requires long history?)
\item \textbf{Use.} Does the model use the structure for prediction, or is it merely detectable?
(Charter priority~2.) A third charter priority asks whether some topological distinctions
are unnecessary for the next symbol but necessary for a longer future.
\end{enumerate}
Two secondary programmes extend the question: does the world's geometry change learning speed,
and can a model use topology richer than $H_1$, namely non-abelian transport, where the
\emph{order} in which loops are traversed matters? Table~\ref{tab:key} in
\S\ref{sec:programme} lists every study by a descriptive name; the short internal labels of the
project records (for example K4b or X6S1) appear only there and in the appendix tables, as keys
for readers who want to look up a record in the repository.

The project is also an experiment in research process. I froze every decision rule before
the data it decides existed. Each protocol was planned twice independently and reviewed
result-blind before freezing, and the critical tests were written by authors who never saw the
implementation. Compute is metered, fresh test histories are protected by exposure registries,
and failures are classified as technical (correctable at most twice), inconclusive, or
scientific. I did much of the work with language-model agents: OpenAI Codex and Anthropic
Claude models served as independent planners, result-blind reviewers, implementers and test
authors, and a Claude session acted as coordinator (issuing rulings and writing reviews under my
authority) with workers launched per job, all communicating through a version-controlled
mailbox. I approved every scientific decision point.

\paragraph{Summary of findings.} Table~\ref{tab:headline} lists the main outcomes;
Figure~\ref{fig:fig01_timeline} places all of them in time.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{RQ1, positive under the smooth emission O.} The preregistered F1 rule is supported
in every panel and replicate; the detected loop is the world's periodic circle (\S\ref{sec:pilot}).
\item \textbf{RQ2, partially answered.} The geometry follows the full-history belief, also among
histories with the same last symbol, and the learned circle exceeds an optimistic last-symbol
bound. But the last-symbol embedding already carries much of the contrast, the timing
hypothesis was rejected, beliefs converge quickly with history length, and the most direct
test found the beyond-four-symbol covariance of the learned phase to be smaller than
0.01---a margin that the oracle's own phase also does not reach (\S\ref{sec:origin}).
\item \textbf{RQ3, answered for the registered code.} The first two intervention designs,
erasing the circular coordinate and forcing a counterfactual state, stopped at preregistered
feasibility gates before any causal endpoint was scored. The third design attributes the
model's own response to natural counterfactual histories exactly to its circle code at the
final readout: the code carries 85\% of the oracle-aligned response in all three models, and
the preregistered outcome is USED (\S\ref{sec:causal}). A follow-up on fresh histories confirmed
that the code is the main carrier but not the only one, and showed on decoy models that the
method returns ``not used'' when the code is present but unread (\S\ref{sec:c2nd}).
\item \textbf{Horizons and richer topology.} Exact oracle computations prove the existence of an
orientability distinction that is invisible one step ahead and needed two steps ahead, but the
trained-model study of it failed its learnability gate. An $S_3$ transport process was
certified to carry order information beyond every commutative statistic; the
trained follow-up reached its preregistered decision, INCONCLUSIVE: a weak but
reproducible natural order sensitivity, far below the registered size (\S\ref{sec:horizons},
\S\ref{sec:x6}, \S\ref{sec:x6s1results}). A follow-up check left the cause of one control's
response to the designed placebo unexplained (\S\ref{sec:x6zc}).
\item \textbf{A world with more history to measure.} An oracle-only sweep of 225 worlds selected
one in which the oracle's beyond-four-symbol signal is 4.7 times the registered margin, so that
the origin question can be asked again with room to answer it (\S\ref{sec:hws}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and related work}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Transformers, hidden Markov models and belief states}
An HMM with transition matrix $T$ and emission matrix $E$ induces, for every observed history,
a posterior over latent states: the belief state, obtained by Bayesian filtering
\citep{rabiner1989tutorial}. The set of reachable beliefs, together with the map from belief
to future-symbol distributions, is the object studied by computational mechanics, where the
minimal sufficient statistic of the past for the future (the causal state, or
$\varepsilon$-machine state) is the natural notion of what an optimal predictor must remember
\citep{crutchfield1989inferring,shalizi2001computational}. Predictive-state and
observable-operator representations express the same information through future-observation
statistics \citep{jaeger2000oom,singh2004psr}. Shai et al.\ showed that transformers trained
on small edge-emitting HMMs linearly encode the belief-state simplex, including fractal belief
geometries, in the residual stream, sometimes only in the concatenation of layers
\citep{shai2024belief}; Piotrowski et al.\ explained the layer-wise geometry as constrained
belief updates by attention followed by nonlinear correction \citep{piotrowski2025constrained}.
Related accounts treat in-context learning as implicit Bayesian inference over latent concepts
\citep{xie2022icl}, show how transformers learn Markov-chain statistics in context through
induction-like heads with stage-wise dynamics \citep{edelman2024statistical,nichani2024causal,bietti2023birth},
and study what function classes can be learned in context \citep{garg2022incontext}. More
recent preprints report factored representations when latent factors are independent
\citep{shai2026factored} and belief geometry in pretrained models in context
\citep{balcells2026llm}.

\emph{Relation to this project.} The pilot uses exactly this oracle machinery (\S\ref{sec:process}):
the exact filter supplies the 32-symbol belief $\bel{32}$, the last-symbol posterior $\bel{1}$
and the future laws $F_1,F_2$, and every learned geometry is compared with these
model-free spaces on the same histories. Two design choices come directly from this
literature. First, the axis-wise emission permutation P (\S\ref{sec:emissions}) preserves the
exact product structure of the smooth emission, because factor-wise representations are
expected \citep{shai2026factored}. Second, layer-wise and concatenated hooks are analysed
because belief geometry need not sit in one layer \citep{shai2024belief,piotrowski2025constrained}.
The pilot's addition is a controlled \emph{topological} contrast at matched state count, and
persistent homology on clouds of individual histories rather than on per-state centroids.

\subsection{World models, emergent geometry and in-context representations}
Othello-playing sequence models carry recoverable board state \citep{li2023othello}; models
trained on random walks can predict well without a coherent world model by stricter metrics
\citep{vafa2024worldmodel}; pretrained language models represent space and time linearly
\citep{gurnee2024space} and some features are irreducibly multi-dimensional, including
circular ones such as days of the week \citep{engels2025notlinear}. Pretrained models
reorganise their representations to match a graph defined in context, but the analysis there
uses per-node averages, whose principal components reproduce the spectral embedding of the
graph \citep{park2025iclr}. Closest to the pilot in spirit, Jerpelea planted a ring-structured
latent Markov variable in generated text and found ring geometry in a from-scratch student,
using centroids and PCA \citep{jerpelea2026planting}; Brenner et al.\ studied fully observed
lattice walkers, whose residual stream encodes running averages of steps
\citep{brenner2026grid}.

\emph{Relation.} These results make two pitfalls concrete. Per-state averages import the world's
structure from outside the model, and short-range symbol mixing alone can create global
lattice structure. The pilot therefore forbids centroids, UMAP/t-SNE and $k$-NN geodesics as
evidence, and adds two model-free mixing baselines (previous-token mixing of a spectral
symbol embedding, and the context average), together with the trained last-symbol embedding
(\fpath{docs/RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md} \S7.2, \S7.6). The claim of the pilot is accordingly narrow:
the general phenomenon ``periodic latent dynamics can shape periodic geometry'' has prior art
\citep{jerpelea2026planting}; the pilot contributes a matched two-topology comparison with these
controls. I do not claim priority.

\subsection{Formal languages, automata, groups and state tracking}
Theoretical work bounds what fixed-depth self-attention can compute: hard and soft attention
cannot robustly recognise parity or Dyck languages at arbitrary length \citep{hahn2020theoretical},
empirical studies map the formal languages transformers learn \citep{bhattamishra2020ability},
and a Chomsky-hierarchy benchmark finds transformers weak at state tracking beyond regular
languages without structural help \citep{deletang2023chomsky}. Log-precision transformers lie in
uniform $\mathrm{TC}^0$ \citep{merrill2023parallelism}; since the word problem of $S_5$ is
$\mathrm{NC}^1$-complete by Barrington's theorem \citep{barrington1989bounded}, neither
transformers nor common state-space models can track general non-solvable group state at
arbitrary length unless $\mathrm{TC}^0=\mathrm{NC}^1$ \citep{merrill2024illusion,sarrof2024expressive};
negative eigenvalues restore some state tracking in linear RNNs \citep{grazzi2025unlocking}.
For \emph{solvable} groups, transformers learn shallow ``shortcuts'' whose existence follows from
the Krohn--Rhodes decomposition \citep{liu2023shortcuts,krohn1965algebraic}. Empirical
mechanistic work shows that networks learn group operations through representation-theoretic
circuits \citep{chughtai2023toy}, that language models track permutation state with associative
and parity-associative mechanisms \citep{li2025track}, and a recent preprint argues that standard
transformers learn cosets of normal subgroups \citep{zhang2026cosets}.

\emph{Relation.} The crossing-order studies rest on this distinction. Their transport group $S_3$ is
non-abelian but solvable, so the composition task is expressible at fixed depth
\citep{liu2023shortcuts}, so expressivity is not in doubt; the question is whether next-token training on a
\emph{topologically generated, implicitly observed} group word yields order-sensitive predictions
beyond commutative summaries. The commutative competitors form a ladder (parity, which is
the quotient $S_3/A_3$; signed crossing counts, i.e.\ the $H_1$ class; unsigned counts; the full
Parikh vector of directed crossings, the strongest commutative statistic of a word
\citep{parikh1966context}). The coset account predicts either full order use or parity-level
behaviour for $S_3$, whose normal subgroups are trivial, $A_3$ and $S_3$ \citep{zhang2026cosets};
the trained crossing-order protocol records this as a named competing hypothesis, checked
against the preprint's abstract only.

\subsection{Order sensitivity versus bag-of-words}
Transformer language models use much less word-order information than their architecture
permits: ablating order within long-range context changes little \citep{oconnor2021context}, and
pretraining on shuffled text retains much downstream performance \citep{sinha2021masked}.
\emph{Relation.} The crossing-order studies make the order question exact: on designed history
families with identical token and directed-bigram multisets, the oracle's next-symbol law still
differs, so any bag-of-tokens predictor has a certified excess loss (\S\ref{sec:x6s0}). On the
natural distribution, however, bag mechanisms are \emph{not} excluded by the trained study's
design, and the protocol says so.

\subsection{Mechanistic interpretability, probing and interventions}
Circuit-level analysis of transformers \citep{elhage2021framework}, induction heads
\citep{olsson2022induction} and superposition \citep{elhage2022superposition} supply the
vocabulary of residual stream, heads and readouts used here. Linear probes \citep{alain2016probes}
measure decodability, not use; control tasks and selectivity are needed to interpret them
\citep{hewitt2019control,belinkov2022probing}. Linear concept erasure
\citep{ravfogel2020inlp,belrose2023leace} removes linearly decodable information but may leave
nonlinearly decodable information; causal claims require interventions such as interchange
interventions, causal abstraction or activation patching
\citep{geiger2021causal,meng2022locating,zhang2024patching,heimersheim2024patching}.

\emph{Relation.} The pilot's probes compare decoding of $\bel{32}$ from activations against
decoding from the exact next-symbol law and from a one-hot last symbol, which acts as a
competitor-matched control in the spirit of \citet{hewitt2019control}. Both of the project's
causal designs met the known failure modes of interventions: the targeted linear eraser
removed linear recoverability while a nonlinear (RBF) decoder still recovered the coordinate at
0.998, and no random eraser matched its damage; the state-forcing direction shifted protected
readouts that matched controls left nearly untouched (\S\ref{sec:causal}). The third design
uses interchange of natural histories at a site where the readout is exactly linear.

\subsection{Grokking and learning dynamics}
Small transformers on algorithmic tasks can generalise long after fitting the training data
\citep{power2022grokking}; mechanistic progress measures reveal a gradual circuit formation
behind apparently sudden transitions \citep{nanda2023progress}, and different runs can implement
different algorithms for the same modular task \citep{zhong2023clock}. \emph{Relation.} H4 asks a
timing question of this kind (does topology appear only after history use begins?), and the
trained crossing-order study trains for 30,000 updates because the orientability study's
10,000-update recipe showed a flat dev loss from step 1,000; I chose the longer run; it is not a
tuned value.

\subsection{Topological data analysis of representations}
Persistent homology tracks homology classes across a filtration
\citep{edelsbrunner2002persistence,zomorodian2005computing,carlsson2009topology}; diagrams are
stable under perturbation \citep{cohensteiner2007stability}, admit confidence sets
\citep{fasy2014confidence}, and circular coordinates can be extracted from persistent
cohomology classes \citep{desilva2011circular}. Vietoris--Rips barcodes are computed efficiently
by Ripser \citep{bauer2021ripser}; universality of random persistence diagrams is conjectured or
proved under specific conditions \citep{bobrowski2022universality}. Persistent homology has
revealed toroidal structure in grid-cell populations \citep{gardner2022toroidal}, tracked
topology through deep networks \citep{naitzat2020topology,rieck2019neural}, and been applied to
language-model activations, where matched Gaussian clouds produce hundreds of spurious $H_1$
bars and pseudoreplication must be avoided \citep{malhotra2026tracking}; another study argues
that transformer token clouds within a sentence are topologically simple \citep{fitz2026hidden}.
Sheaves and local systems formalise local-to-global consistency \citep{hansen2019sheaves} and
have entered neural architectures \citep{bodnar2022sheaf}; local systems and holonomy are
classical \citep{hatcher2002algebraic}.

\emph{Relation.} The pilot computes Vietoris--Rips homology over $\mathbb{F}_2$ on clouds of 512
individual histories, normalises the longest finite $H_1$ bar by a cloud scale, and reports a
Gaussian floor as context, never as a threshold. Following \citet{hurlbert1984pseudoreplication}
and \citet{malhotra2026tracking}, panels within a run are not treated as replicates; the decision
uses sign consistency across three seeds and eight disjoint panels. The ground truth is computed
from an explicit cubical complex, because the transition graph of the square has 121 independent
cycles that are filled faces, not holes. The crossing-order process uses a local system (a flat $S_3$ connection with one
non-flat face) in the \emph{generator}; nothing is claimed about the trained model being a sheaf.

\subsection{Concentration bounds and resampling}
The bootstrap \citep{efron1979bootstrap} is used throughout for descriptive intervals and
several confirmatory rules, with stated nominal (not distribution-free) coverage. Where a
finite-sample guarantee is needed, the trained crossing-order study uses an empirical-Bernstein bound for a bounded
loss: variance-adaptive bounds of this type are due to \citet{audibert2009exploration} and
\citet{maurer2009empirical}, in contrast to the variance-blind Hoeffding inequality
\citep{hoeffding1963probability}; see \citet{boucheron2013concentration} for background. The
protocol derives its own version with a paired U-statistic for the variance, at a small cost
in constants relative to \citet{maurer2009empirical} (\S\ref{sec:x6s1}).

\subsection{Preregistration, reproducibility and leakage}
Undisclosed analytic flexibility inflates false positives \citep{simmons2011false};
preregistration separates confirmatory from exploratory analysis
\citep{nosek2018preregistration,munafo2017manifesto}. In machine learning, variance across seeds
and tooling undermines single-run claims \citep{henderson2018matters,bouthillier2021variance,zhuang2021randomness},
and reproducibility programmes have been proposed \citep{pineau2021reproducibility}. Data leakage
and test contamination are a recurrent source of invalid results
\citep{kaufman2012leakage,kapoor2023leakage,magar2022contamination,sainz2023contamination}.
\emph{Relation.} The methods of \S\ref{sec:methods} are meant as a strict response to these
problems: frozen rules, result-blind review, three-valued outcomes, exposure registries for fresh test
histories, sealed validation quantities, and bitwise rerun checks.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{The synthetic processes and the exact oracle}\label{sec:process}
\paragraph{Worlds.} A $12\times12$ lattice with 4-neighbourhood, $N=144$ states. The square has
no identification; the cylinder is periodic in $x$. Ground-truth Betti numbers are computed from
an explicit cubical complex over $\mathbb{F}_2$: square $\beta=(1,0,0)$, cylinder $(1,1,0)$. The
primary metric is the intrinsic flat metric
$d_M((x,y),(x',y'))=\sqrt{\min(|x-x'|,12-|x-x'|)^2+(y-y')^2}$ on the cylinder (without the
minimum on the square).

\paragraph{Dynamics.} A lazy walk normalised by the maximal degree,
\begin{equation}
T_{ij}=\frac{1-p_{\mathrm{stay}}}{D_{\max}}\ (i\sim j),\qquad
T_{ii}=1-\deg(i)\,\frac{1-p_{\mathrm{stay}}}{D_{\max}},\qquad p_{\mathrm{stay}}=0.2,\ D_{\max}=4,
\end{equation}
which is symmetric and doubly stochastic, so the stationary law $\pi$ is uniform.

\paragraph{Emissions.}\label{sec:emissions} Four conditions (\fpath{docs/RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md} \S4):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{O} (main): 36 symbols with centres $c_{ab}=(0.5+2a,0.5+2b)$ on a $6\times6$ grid,
$E_{zj}\propto\exp(-d_M(z,c_j)^2/2\sigma^2)$, symbol names randomly permuted; $\sigma=1.25$ was
the first candidate to pass the generator-only calibration rule. O factorises exactly as
$E^x[x,a]\,E^y[y,b]$.
\item \textbf{P} (matched control): $E_P[(x,y)]=E_O[(\sigma_x(x),\sigma_y(y))]$ with fixed axis
permutations, preserving rows, entropies, symbol marginals and the product structure while
destroying spatial smoothness.
\item \textbf{N} (negative control): $E_{zw}=1/36$, nothing is learnable about the state.
\item \textbf{U} (sanity control): one symbol per state ($V=144$).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Sequences and the exact predictor.} $z_1\sim\pi$; for $t=1,\dots,33$ emit $w_t\sim E[z_t]$
and transition. The model sees $[\mathrm{BOS},w_1,\dots,w_{32}]$ and predicts $w_{t+1}$ at every
position. The filter, in row vectors and float64, is
\begin{equation}
a_1=\pi,\qquad b_t=\frac{a_t\odot E_{:,w_t}}{a_tE_{:,w_t}},\qquad a_{t+1}=b_tT,\qquad q_t=b_tTE .
\end{equation}
From it come $\bel{32}=b_{32}$, the last-symbol posterior $\bel{1}=r_{w_{32}}$ with
$r_w=\pi\odot E_{:,w}/(\pi E_{:,w})$, the next-symbol law $F_1=bTE$ and the two-symbol law
$F_2[i,j]=b\,T\,\mathrm{diag}(E_{:,i})\,T\,E_{:,j}$. The \emph{context gain} measures what the full
history adds to the last symbol, and the \emph{learning gate} measures how much of it the model
captures:
\begin{equation}
\GC=\overline{\KL(q_C\,\|\,q_{\mathrm{last}})},\qquad
\mathrm{regret}=\overline{\KL(q_C\,\|\,p_{\mathrm{model}})},\qquad
\mathrm{captured}=1-\mathrm{regret}/\GC\ \ge\ 0.8 .
\end{equation}
Later studies reuse the same filter and sampler on other worlds: a $4\times3$ cylinder/M\"obius
seam band with a hidden world bit (the orientability studies), two $12\times12$ tori with a
Dehn-twisted emission (the component-identity study), and a $4\times4$ torus carrying an $S_3$
local system (the crossing-order studies).

\subsection{The transformer learners}
Every trained model in the project uses the same pilot architecture
(\fpath{docs/RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md} \S6): a decoder-only pre-LN transformer \citep{vaswani2017attention,xiong2020layer}
with 4 blocks, $d=128$, 4 heads, MLP width 512, GELU, learned positional embeddings, final
LayerNorm, untied unembedding, no dropout, about 0.8M parameters (802,304 in the crossing-order
study, with 18 output symbols). Training uses AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019adamw} with learning rate $10^{-3}$, 200 warm-up
steps, cosine decay, weight decay 0.1, clipping 1.0 and batch 128. The pilot trained for 5,000
updates on 131,072 trajectories per replicate, with checkpoints at 0, 50, 100, 200, 400, 700,
1,000, 1,500, 2,000, 3,000, 4,000 and 5,000; seeds 1729, 2718 and 3141 change data,
initialisation, symbol names and batch order together. The primary hook is \fpath{resid_post_3},
the residual stream after the last block and before the final LayerNorm. The orientability
study trained 10,000 updates (with optional direct joint heads for 2- and 3-symbol futures), and
the crossing-order study 30,000 updates on $2^{20}$ histories per model.

\subsection{Measurement: persistent homology and the F1 rule}
For each replicate, eight disjoint panels of 512 test histories (one per trajectory, at position
32) are shared by every hook, checkpoint and space. On each cloud a Vietoris--Rips filtration
over $\mathbb{F}_2$ in dimensions 0--1 is computed, and
\begin{equation}
S=\max_{\text{finite }H_1\text{ bars}}\frac{\mathrm{death}-\mathrm{birth}}{s},\qquad s=q_{95}\{d(x,x')>0\}.
\end{equation}
For replicate $r$ and panel $i$, the contrast removes what the architecture produces at
initialisation and what training produces in either world:
\begin{equation}
D_{r,i}=(S^{\mathrm{cyl}}_{\mathrm{final},i}-S^{\mathrm{cyl}}_{\mathrm{init},i})-(S^{\mathrm{sq}}_{\mathrm{final},i}-S^{\mathrm{sq}}_{\mathrm{init},i}),
\qquad u_r=\max_c q_{95}\{|S_{c,i}-S_{c,j}|:i<j\},
\end{equation}
with $c$ ranging over the four cells. \textbf{F1 is supported} iff $D_{r,i}>0$ in at least 7 of 8
panels in each of the three replicates, $\mathrm{median}_iD_{r,i}>u_r$ for each replicate, and
every model passed the learning gate; it is rejected iff all gates passed and a condition failed;
otherwise it is inconclusive. H2 uses
$\dgeom=\rho_s(d_A,d_{B32})-\rho_s(d_A,d_{B1})$ over all pairs, and again over pairs with the same
$w_{32}$, decided by sign consistency; distances on beliefs are Hellinger. Pair correlations are
never significance tests.

\subsection{Preregistration and frozen protocols}
The pilot's hypotheses, measures and rules were committed in \fpath{PREREGISTRATION.md} after
the oracle analysis and before any training; secondary analyses were registered blind in
\fpath{PREREGISTRATION_X4.md}; every operational detail of the last pilot stage was fixed before
any of its results existed. A change to a closed decision required my approval at a checkpoint;
the one such change was the addition of the belief-alignment hypothesis \Hp\ before any result
under P existed. Each follow-up protocol is frozen by its SHA-256 hash; coordinator addenda may
fix implementation footprint or bindings but may not change a gate, threshold, seed or decision
rule.
Every frozen protocol contains a threshold register, an outcome taxonomy with a licensed
sentence for each outcome, and an explicit ``never licensed'' list. Three-valued outcomes
(PASS / INCONCLUSIVE / FAIL\_TECHNICAL, or the study-specific equivalents) prevent a technical
failure from being read as a negative finding. Since the counterfactual-forcing study, protocols
also include a \emph{tolerance-attainability} table fixed before data (\S\ref{sec:causal}),
and since the four-symbol origin test an \emph{oracle-scale check} that compares margins with
what the oracle itself achieves (\S\ref{sec:origin}).

\subsection{Dual-track planning and independent review}
From 14 October I had every planning task done twice, independently, under a written planning
protocol: one track by Codex in a read-only sandbox and the other by a clean-context Claude
subagent, with identical request files and base commits and
no access to each other's output. The coordinator wrote a merge document recording agreements,
conflicts, the decision on each and what each track caught that the other missed. A third,
result-blind LLM review (Codex, or a clean-context Claude reviewer when Codex was unavailable) had to
return its required changes, and a confirmation pass had to return CONFIRM\_FOR\_FREEZE before
any freeze. For example, the counterfactual-forcing protocol went through 13, then 7, then 4
required corrections; the trained crossing-order protocol through 14 required changes, including
a verification of the empirical-Bernstein proof. The merge of the two counterfactual-forcing
tracks found that half of one track's ``random controls'' exactly reproduced the primary
intervention.

\subsection{Independent test authors}
For critical modules the tests are written from a written API contract (\fpath{API.md}) by a
separate LLM author who never reads the implementation, and they are committed before the module enters the
repository; the trained crossing-order study and the readout-attribution study used two
independent authors. Corrections to an author's tests are
made by the same author or a clean-context successor that reads only the governing ruling and
the test, never module code. The implementer never writes the acceptance tests.
The coordinator independently reruns the full test suite at each code freeze (e.g.\ 4,288 passed,
0 failed, at the code freeze of the trained crossing-order study).

\subsection{Metered compute}
Every scientific process runs under a meter that places the process tree in a Windows Job
Object and records child CPU including exited descendants, meter CPU and a 0.1\,s tail
allowance per run into a store-local \fpath{cpu_ledger.json}. CPU is \emph{measure-only} in the
later studies (no CPU ceiling; a wall limit and memory limits guard against runaway jobs).
The orientability training study revealed that its per-model supervisor had watched the
virtual-environment launcher, not the training interpreter, so per-process CPU read about 0;
the job-level meter was unaffected. A repair moved accounting to the whole process tree, and
the crossing-order study's preflight checks that a deliberately over-limit child is killed with
its training interpreter.

\subsection{Registries against data leakage}
A fresh test set is fresh only if none of its histories were ever generated, read or used for
fitting in an earlier stage. From the counterfactual-forcing study onwards, each study builds an
\emph{exposure registry} of digests of every history that any earlier study could have exposed
(3.36 million digests for the counterfactual-forcing study; 6,024,521 for the four-symbol origin
test; about 19.7 million new ones plus the latter for the crossing-order study), and separation
gates refuse a fresh pool with any collision. Reservations forbid any other history-generating
job while a registry is being built. Test scratch directories inside the project root once made
a registry inventory incomplete; scratch now lives outside the root.

\subsection{Sealing until the decision stage}
Quantities that could influence later choices are written to \fpath{sealed/} with their hashes
and released only at the decision stage or at a stop. In the four-symbol origin test the
validation means stayed sealed until the fresh result was recorded; in the trained crossing-order
study the learnability-gate statistics, the learning curves and the headroom diagnostics were
sealed, and the gate record released only PASS/FAIL/UNRESOLVED and the decision. Any model
prediction on test histories before the decision stage is defined as leakage. This document
follows the same rule: no model-performance number from that study was reported in earlier
versions, and the numbers in \S\ref{sec:x6s1results} come only from the decision-stage report
written after unsealing.

\subsection{The technical-correction rules}
A documented implementation or environment defect may be corrected by a coordinator ruling
that names the defect, the correction commit, the affected artifacts and the evidence of
independence from the outcome, with \emph{at most two correction jobs per defect}. The failed
attempt moves append-only to \fpath{superseded/<job>-a<k>/}; unaffected artifacts must regenerate
bit-identically. A failure that shows a protocol statement to be wrong is not a defect: it
stops the study and returns to planning. Any post-data change of a threshold or tolerance needs
the owner. Environment reruns that reproduce identical values do not count as corrections, and
final outcomes are never rerun.

\section{The pilot}\label{sec:pilot}

\subsection{Validating the measurement on known topology}
\textbf{Question.} Does the persistent-homology measurement detect a surface hole, as opposed to
graph cycles? \textbf{Design.} A gate with thresholds fixed before any measurement: correct Betti
numbers; $S_{\mathrm{cyl}}\ge0.20$ and $S_{\mathrm{sq}}<0.10$ on $d_M$; synthetic circle versus disk
clouds ($M=512$ in $\mathbb{R}^{128}$) classified correctly in at least 19 of 20 draws.
\textbf{Result.} $S$ = 0.299 (cylinder) and 0.037 (square) on $d_M$; 0.231 and 0.067 on the graph
metric, although the transition graphs have 133 and 121 independent cycles; synthetic calibration
20/20 (mean circle $S$ 0.460, maximum disk $S$ 0.045). The gate passed (\fpath{reports/E0/summary.md}).
\textbf{Lesson.} Graph cycles are not surface holes; the measurement targets the latter.

\subsection{The topological difference exists in the exact belief}
\textbf{Question.} Do the model-free spaces separate the worlds? \textbf{Result.} Under O every space
separates the worlds in 8/8 panels, at 3--8 times the Gaussian floor; the full belief separates
most strongly (0.671 vs 0.321). Under P the exact belief barely separates (5/8 panels), and the
future spaces reverse (Figure~\ref{fig:fig03_e1_oracle_spaces}). The last-symbol posterior and
the emission field are identical under O and P, because P permutes states.
\textbf{Interpretation.} RQ1 is decidable under O, and history strengthens the expected signature.
But H3 (``the structure survives under P'') became \emph{one-sided} before any P model was trained:
a failure under P would be expected even of the ideal predictor. This reading was registered
before the P runs.

\subsection{The main experiment under the smooth emission O}
\textbf{Design.} Six runs (2 worlds $\times$ 3 seeds) plus one N model, 13--16 CPU minutes each.
\textbf{What ran.} All six passed the learning gate (captured 0.985--0.986 for the cylinder and
0.992 for the square; regret 0.0018--0.0039 nats against $\GC$ = 0.2649 / 0.2360).
\textbf{Result.} F1 \textbf{supported}: $D>0$ in 8/8 panels in each replicate, median $D$ = 0.158,
0.176, 0.173 against $u_r$ = 0.097, 0.084, 0.084 (Figure~\ref{fig:fig04_f1_contrast}a). At
initialisation the worlds are indistinguishable ($S\approx0.06$); after training the cylinder
reaches $S\approx0.31$ and the square $\approx0.13$. H2 is sign-consistent: $\dgeom>0$ in 48/48
panels over all pairs (medians 0.087--0.127), and among same-$w_{32}$ pairs, where the
last-symbol embedding is constant, the activation distances track $\bel{32}$ with $\rho_s$
0.935--0.979. The N model sits at $H(W)=\ln36$ (NLL 3.5835, half-width 0.0002): no leakage. In a
secondary analysis over six views of the final state the F1 rule holds in all six, including
the component the readout cannot see (\fpath{reports/E2/summary.md}).
\textbf{The last-symbol limitation.} The trained embedding of the last symbol alone reproduces
0.89, 0.47 and 0.54 of the model's median $D$; the model-free mixing baselines
(previous-token mixing and the context average) stay at $\le0.20$
(Figure~\ref{fig:fig04_f1_contrast}b). F1 alone therefore does not show that history
integration produces the hole.

\subsection{The permuted control P and training dynamics}
\textbf{P.} All six P runs passed the learning gate (captured 0.874--0.914). The F1 rule was
\textbf{rejected} (median $D$ 0.021--0.034 below $u_r$ 0.066--0.089; 6/8 and 5/8 positive panels in
two replicates), which, as preregistered, is uninformative. The last-symbol-embedding contrast
collapses under P (replicate 1729: 0.140 $\to$ $-0.035$), so its contrast under O is not static
emission geometry; but
whether it reflects history integration or learned symbol-level transition statistics remains
open. \textbf{\Hp\ supported}: under P, $\rho_s(d_A,d_{B32})$ rises from 0.06--0.11 at
initialisation to 0.70 and exceeds the better mixing baseline (0.39) in 48/48 run-panels.
\emph{Learned representations can align with Bayesian belief geometry even when the registered
$H_1$ contrast is not supported}; an $H_1$ contrast is expected only when the belief geometry
itself carries the hole.

\textbf{H4 (timing).} With $t_h$ the first checkpoint where regret $<\GC$ and $t_s$ the first where

LayerNorm ($\approx0.28$), with P near zero at every hook (Figure~\ref{fig:fig06_layer_sweep}).

\subsection{Controls, probes, circular coordinates, sensitivity and a clean rerun}
\textbf{Controls.} Two further N models sit at $\ln36$ within $2\times10^{-5}$. The U models learn the
visible-state dynamics (captured$_U$ 0.999) and also form an $x$-aligned circle (agreement 0.601),
showing that symbol-level transition statistics alone can produce such a circle.
\textbf{Probes.} Ridge probes fitted on dev panels decode $\bel{32}$ from activations with test $R^2$
0.829 under O---no better than from the exact next-symbol law $F_1$ (0.838), far above
initialisation (0.483) and the one-hot last symbol (0.421); the shuffled-label control is near zero
(Figure~\ref{fig:fig07_probes_circular}a). The preregistered pattern (activations above all three
competitors) held in 1/6 runs per condition. Under O the selected ridge $\lambda$ was the smallest
grid value in every run, so $R^2$ may be slightly underestimated; the grid was frozen.
\textbf{Circular coordinates.} From the leading $H_1$ cocycle (over $\mathbb{F}_{47}$, at
mid-lifetime) a circular coordinate $\varphi$ is computed and compared with the latent angle
$\theta=2\pi x(z_{32})/12$ by
$A=\max(|\overline{e^{i(\varphi-\theta)}}|,|\overline{e^{i(\varphi+\theta)}}|)$. Cylinder models under O
reach 0.884--0.892 (initialisation 0.06--0.12; non-periodic $y$ 0.10; square 0.21--0.30; exact
belief 0.915; Figure~\ref{fig:fig07_probes_circular}b). This identifies \emph{which} loop F1
measures; it is privileged-evaluation and descriptive. A later diagnostic noted that the
construction can silently give off-giant-component points an arbitrary offset; at most one point
per panel was affected.

\subsection{Origin beyond the last four symbols: does the learned phase covary with that information?}
\textbf{Question.} In the pilot's three cylinder models under O (step 5000, \fpath{resid_post_3}, position 32), does
the circular coordinate $u_j(h)=(\cos\varphi_j,\sin\varphi_j)$ built by the new chart rule on fresh
landmarks covary with $r_4=m_{32}-m_4$, the part of the first-harmonic belief that the last four
observations do not determine? \textbf{Estimand.} $\Delta_4=\E[u\cdot r_4]$. By the tower property
$\Delta_4=0$ for every suffix-only coordinate. Rows without chart support contribute the interval
$[-\|r_4\|,+\|r_4\|]$, so $[\E X^-,\E X^+]$ is exactly the identified set over all completions.
\textbf{Decision.} Per model, SUPPORT iff $L(\E X^-)-F>0.01$; EXCLUDE iff $U(\E X^+)+F<0.01$ and
$L(\E X^-)-F>-0.01$, with one-sided bounds at $0.05/3$ and a numerical margin
$F=\max(B_{\mathrm{num}},10^{-9})+Z_{\mathrm{bound}}$ that makes the claim concern the documented
law. \textbf{What ran.} The calibration and validation stage needed three technical
corrections, each within the two-per-defect rule: a dictionary passed to \texttt{Fraction}, a
byte-order mark and a lazy sequence in the registry handoff, and a missing reader for the
store's own superseded digests; no outcome-bearing quantity existed at any failure point.
Validation then passed every gate (support 3072/3072 in every model, oracle-headroom lower
bound 0.0636) and fixed $N=3072$ test rows. \textbf{Result.} On the fresh test,
\textbf{EXCLUDE in all three models}: $[L,U]$ = [0.00640, 0.00777], [0.00740, 0.00891],
[0.00667, 0.00823] (Figure~\ref{fig:fig08_origin_profile_k4b}b). Suffix-only nulls were near zero,
as designed.

\textbf{Interpretation and caveat.} The licensed sentence is that, for this chart rule, site and
process, a covariance of either sign at or beyond 0.01 is excluded. But the phase-only oracle
comparator itself achieves only $\Delta_4^{orc}=0.00854$ [0.00791, 0.00933], below the margin, and
the models reach 83--95\% of it; all lower bounds are positive. EXCLUSION therefore means only
that the covariance is small in absolute terms; it does not show that the learned phase lacks
beyond-four-symbol information.
The protocol registered no ``positive but below margin'' status, so no stronger claim is licensed.
\textbf{Lesson.} Margins must be checked against the oracle's own achievable value before
freezing; the trained crossing-order and readout-attribution protocols adopted this as an
oracle-scale check.

\subsection{Component identity beyond short windows (oracle only)}
\textbf{Question.} In a process whose components have identical single-symbol statistics, how
much component information is available from short windows versus the full history, and is the
full history more than a product of block likelihoods? \textbf{Design.} Two $12\times12$ tori with
the pilot walk; component B emits A's field pulled back by the Dehn twist
$\phi(x,y)=(x+y \bmod 12,y)$; exact enumeration for windows up to 4 and $2^{20}$ validated Monte
Carlo sequences otherwise. \textbf{Result.} GO (full history needed, non-additively): the last
symbol carries no component information; window information rises from 0.002 nats (2 symbols)
to 0.067 (8) and 0.325 (full history); full-history Bayes accuracy exceeds the 8-window accuracy by
0.191 (99\% interval [0.190, 0.192]) and a four-block likelihood product by 0.0567 [0.0562, 0.0571], above the 0.03
threshold; a local-noise control was nearly additive (0.008).
\textbf{Interpretation.} GO licenses \emph{designing} a trained stage only, about component identity
rather than the circular coordinate; short windows are not blind. It motivated the search for a
construction where short windows are \emph{exactly} blind, which became the crossing-order
studies (\S\ref{sec:x6}).

\subsection{A sweep of worlds with more history to measure (oracle only)}\label{sec:hws}
\textbf{Question.} The origin test beyond four symbols registered a margin of 0.01 that the
oracle's own beyond-four quantity in the pilot world (0.0085) does not reach. Is there a
cylinder world, close to the pilot's, in which that quantity is at least three times the margin,
and in which context helps next-symbol prediction enough that a model could plausibly learn it?
\textbf{Design.} Oracle only: nothing is trained and no model is run. The grid has 225 worlds:
the walk's stay probability (0.2 to 0.9375), the emission width (1.25 to 3), circular aliasing of
the symbols (one to three levels) and the circumference (8, 12 or 16), with height 12 and
histories of 32 symbols. Exact filters removed worlds whose symbol maps are reflection- or
translation-symmetric (75) and worlds whose circle is not legible (43). The rest were screened on
4,096 rows each, and 16 finalists were confirmed on 32,768 fresh rows with simultaneous lower
bounds ($\alpha=0.05/16$). A world qualifies if four lower bounds hold: the beyond-four signal
(the oracle comparator of the origin test) is at least 0.03; the context gain is at least 0.10
nats; the beyond-four predictive gain is at least the pilot world's value and at least 0.0078
nats; and the same holds for the circular part of the symbol. Among qualified worlds the smallest
departure from the pilot world wins.
\textbf{What ran.} The known-case stage needed three attempts. A scope check stopped the first
before anything was sampled. A known case failed the second by one rounding unit on a constant
input, which I settled by decision. The third passed. The sweep itself then passed every stage
in 15 minutes. The study used 0.54 CPU-hours in all, including tests.
\textbf{Result} (\fpath{hws-store/stages/FINAL/REPORT.json}). SELECTED, at the lowest departure
tier: the same $12\times12$ cylinder and the same walk (stay probability 0.2), with emission
width 1.75 instead of 1.25, so no new observation code is needed. On the fresh confirmation rows
the beyond-four signal is 0.0470 (lower bound 0.0458; pilot world 0.0085), 4.7 times the margin.
The lower bounds of the context gain, the beyond-four predictive gain and its circular part
are 0.283, 0.031 and 0.018 nats, against floors of 0.10, 0.012 and 0.006.
\textbf{Licence.} This is an oracle property of one world. It says nothing about whether a model
trained there learns the world or would carry the signal; that is the question of the training
pilot (\S\ref{sec:status}).

\section{Causal use (charter priority 2)}\label{sec:causal}

\subsection{Erasing the circular coordinate}
\textbf{Design.} Erase the direction carrying the coordinate $m_{32}$ at the final site and compare
the damage to prediction with random erasers matched in damage. \textbf{Result.} The targeted eraser
removed linear recoverability, but a fixed RBF decoder still recovered the coordinate at 0.998
(LINEAR\_COMPONENT\_ONLY), and 0 of 10,000 random erasers fell inside the frozen damage-matching

(\fpath{docs/proposals/X4F_COORDINATOR_REVIEW_ADDENDUM.md}). \textbf{Lesson.} The circular information is
nonlinearly and redundantly encoded; linear erasure cannot test its use.

\subsection{Counterfactual state forcing}
\textbf{Question.} At \fpath{resid_post_3}, position 32, of the pilot's cylinder models under O,
where only the final LayerNorm and unembedding follow, does the readout map a change of the
learned chart phase into a change of the predictive distribution in the direction an exact
counterfactual oracle prescribes? Because the unembedding has rank at most 35, the
residual-to-output Jacobian has a kernel of dimension at least 93, so the test is not trivial.
\textbf{Design.} Donors $H_\pm$ shift the oracle state along the circle; a Frisch--Waugh--Lovell
phase map fitted on calibration rows defines the forced state; 32 Haar-random whitened 2-frames
form the matched control family; gates bound damage to the decoded last symbol $\hat w$ and the
decoded $y$-marginal, support, registration of the counterfactual, a positive control,
and a numerical certificate of every oracle row. The first version was blocked by the chart
failure of \S\ref{sec:origin}; a revised version (\Cp) replaced that dependency with the
largest-component chart rule (33 anchored edits).
\textbf{What ran.} The oracle stage first failed technically (an integer/string key mismatch),
then passed every gate on bit-identical histories: the exact belief predicts the forced-state
effect with a one-sided lower bound of 1.12 nats of mean gain, far above 0.01. The calibration
stage first failed at a ridge-equivalence self-check; a coordinator ruling relaxed that
tolerance after oracle-stage data existed, and the rerun reached the damage gate.
\textbf{Result.} INCONCLUSIVE (no matched control family) at the calibration damage gate in all
three models. The primary
intervention flipped the decoded $w_{32}$ on 29--39\% of rows ($D_w(T)$ = 0.311, 0.389, 0.286
against a ceiling of 0.02) and shifted the decoded $y$-marginal by 2.5--3.2 times its calibration
variance (ceiling 0.05), while the matched controls barely moved $\hat w$ ($<0.001$); only 6, 0 and 4
of 32 frames matched, against 28 required. The coordinator found no implementation defect: the gap
is a property of the fitted direction, not of displacement size.
\textbf{Governance flag.} That ruling changed a tolerance after data existed, without my prior
approval, in tension with the protocol's own rules. The damage-gate values do not depend on it
(charts and earlier gate values are bit-identical), and under a strict reading the outcome would
have been FAIL\_TECHNICAL at the self-check, which licenses the same ``assay infeasible as

tolerance-attainability table fixed before data.

\textbf{Interpretation for RQ3.} Neither design scored a causal endpoint. Both failures tell me about
the instruments and nothing about use: the information is nonlinear, and the obvious linear
intervention direction is entangled with the last-symbol readout.

\subsection{Readout attribution of the circle code}
\textbf{Question.} For natural pairs of histories that differ in their $x$-history but share the
last symbol, what share of the model's own oracle-aligned change in next-symbol prediction is
carried by an interchange of its registered circle code at the final position?
\textbf{Design.} After the final LayerNorm the head is exactly linear, so this attribution is
exact and needs no synthetic state passed through a nonlinearity, which removes the failure mode
of the forcing study at its root. The code is the 6-dimensional span of the first three
harmonics of the learned chart phase, made nuisance-excluding by a generalised-least-squares
projector; a secondary family repeats the interchange at layer~2. The share $\psi$ is declared
USED if its lower bound exceeds 0.50 and NOT\_USED if its upper bound is below 0.10 together with
a bound on the total effect; claims need two of the three models.
\textbf{What ran.} After two independent plans, a comparison, a merge and three rounds of
result-blind review I froze the protocol on 21 October, revised it once before any data existed
(a translation placebo on the $y$-axis replaced a reflection that failed on this process), and
authorised execution on 22 October. The first stage needed four attempts: the first three
stopped at entry checks before anything was sampled (a release-record field name; a lineage
check that refused the stage's own entry records; then the same check refusing the compute
meter's own lock file), and each defect was corrected under the two-per-defect rule. From
then on all five stages passed every gate on 26 October: identity, registry and separation,
the exact oracle with Decimal reference rows, eligibility (coverage 0.96 for $x$, 0.88--0.90
for $y$, reproducing a synthetic dry run count for count), model identity, the
generalised-least-squares fits with an 80-digit reference, chart, link and registration
screens on a separate sample, the validation gates (the models respond, the $y$ placebo stays
below its registered bound), and a precision ladder that fixed 4,096 fresh test rows. The whole study used
0.87 CPU-hours.

\textbf{Result} (fresh test, 4,096 rows; \fpath{c2-readout-store/stages/S5/report.json}).
\textbf{USED} in all three models: $\hat\psi=0.851$, 0.854 and 0.856, with one-sided lower
bounds of $\E[A_S-0.50\,A_{\mathrm{full}}]$ of 0.354--0.363 nats, far above zero; the models
respond to the counterfactual ($\theta_{\mathrm{full}}$ 0.95--0.96 against 0.30). The six-dimensional
code carries $\psi\approx0.85$ of the response, while exact random six-dimensional planes of the
127-dimensional readout space carry about 0.03--0.12; the nuisance directions carry about 0.001,
and the rest about 0.145. Its effect is almost entirely visible to the readout. The Euclidean
complement and the one- and two-harmonic codes give the same outcome, so no projector or
harmonic qualifier applies. The layer-2 code interchange is UPSTREAM\_USED: the full layer-2
interchange transmits essentially the whole natural contrast ($\hat\tau_M\approx1.006$), and the
registered layer-2 code carries 0.84 of it, residual skip included. The code built from the
oracle's own phase is also USED ($\hat\psi=0.87$). The validation values, opened only after the
test outcome, agree to about 0.005.

\textbf{Licence and reading.} The outcome concerns the frozen, nuisance-excluding linear code
defined by the fit and projector; it does not exclude predictive use of phase information in
excluded directions, higher harmonics, interactions or other encodings, and it does not show
that the code is the only carrier. Within that scope, the next-symbol readout at the final
position uses the learned circle code for most of its response to the $x$-history. The protocol
stated USED as the expected outcome, so this result confirms an expectation rather than
reporting a surprise. The second-to-last symbol changes with the rest of the $x$-history in
every counterfactual pair, so the result does not separate long history from that symbol.

\subsection{Is the circle code necessary, and can the method say ``not used''?}\label{sec:c2nd}
\textbf{Questions.} (i) Necessity: if everything except the registered code is taken from the
other history (the complement interchange), how much of the response moves? (ii) Specificity:
on a model whose code is present but provably unread, does the same frozen method return
NOT\_USED?
\textbf{Design.} At the final readout the head is exactly linear, so the complement share equals
one minus the code share. It mirrors the previous result rather than adding independent
evidence, but here it is measured on fresh histories. ``Necessary'' (a complement share below
0.10) was therefore not expected; the expected outcome was ``code dominant'' (a complement share
between 0.10 and 0.50). A second family tests necessity at layer~2, where the remaining
computation is non-linear. For specificity, decoy models change only the output (unembedding)
matrix. The code's directions are removed from the readout and the rest is multiplied by a fixed
gain of~6. The code then stays bitwise in the activations, its readout is exactly zero, and the
decoy still responds to the counterfactual. The method re-estimates the code on a fresh split,
and constructed known cases check it on real activations first.
\textbf{What ran.} The study was frozen and authorised on 27 October after five synthetic dry
runs, which exposed and corrected several defects in the code and its rehearsal fixtures. In
the execution, one attempt at the first stage was lost when its background process died before
drawing any data. A later stage started too early and was set aside. Both were rerun, and all
five stages passed every gate. The study used about 5.0 CPU-hours, including tests and dry
runs; the real stages took 1.1.
\textbf{Result} (\fpath{c2nd-store/stages/S5/report.json}; a fresh test of 4,096 rows).
Necessity: CODE\_DOMINANT in all three models. The complement carries 0.149, 0.146 and 0.144 of
the signed oracle-aligned response, so the code is the main carrier of this readout response but
not the only one. The registered qualifiers record that this outcome depends on the projector and
on the first harmonic. At layer~2 the code is also dominant (complement 0.145, 0.149 and 0.147;
interaction share 0.01--0.02, descriptive). Decoys: DECOY\_NOT\_USED in all three. The method
returned code shares of 0.028, 0.026 and 0.020, while the decoys still responded at 0.83--0.84
of the oracle contrast through other directions, and every core known case returned its
constructed answer. Without the gain the decoys fail the response gate, as designed.
\textbf{Reading.} The earlier USED verdict does not come from a method that always says
``used'': on models where the code is present but unread, it says ``not used''. Taken together,
the registered code carries about 85\% of the readout response and everything else 14--15\%, and
the measure is specific on the constructed decoys. The limits stand: only the frozen linear code
is covered, an exact identity ties necessity to the earlier share, and only the constructed
decoys were tested.

\section{Secondary programme: non-abelian transport and crossing order}\label{sec:x6}

\subsection{Crossing order in the oracle: order-dependence certified}\label{sec:x6s0}
\textbf{Construction.} A $4\times4$ torus with the pilot's lazy walk (each move and stay with

cue-normalised TV between members of different holonomy is $\ge0.3$ while every commutative
competitor is constant; (ii) natural: the natural excess log-loss of the best Parikh predictor
$G_{\mathrm{Parikh}}(32)\ge10^{-3}$ nats and the fraction $M_{32}$ of histories with row-KL $\ge0.01$

another gate's estimator), each confirmed by the next run. \textbf{Result.} GO, with the natural
criterion met: witness TVs 0.851, 0.695, 0.524; exact zeros in AB, ZC
and the controls; $G_{\mathrm{Parikh}}(32)=2.636\times10^{-3}$ nats (enclosure width $\sim10^{-60}$)
and $M_{32}=0.0468$; no window of at most 7 symbols carries order information (exact). The ladder
of commutative competitors is in Figure~\ref{fig:fig10_x6_stage0_ladder}.
\textbf{Licence.} Oracle facts about one designed process; a GO supports designing a trained stage,
it does not authorise training. A descriptive note: the literal product-of-experts comparator has a
transport-independent component (about 19 standard errors above zero), so it is not used as
an order signal in the trained study.

\subsection{Crossing order in trained models: do transformers use it?}\label{sec:x6s1}
\textbf{Question.} Do small causal transformers trained only by next-symbol prediction on natural
histories of the non-abelian process (arm NA) make predictions that depend on the order of seam
crossings, beyond the Parikh statistic and its coarsenings? The claim is behavioural; no
mechanism is claimed.

\textbf{Design} (Figure~\ref{fig:fig12_x6s1_design}). Arms NA, AB and ZC $\times$ seeds 1--4: 12
models, each trained for 30,000 updates on $2^{20}$ fresh histories with the pilot architecture and
recipe; for each seed every arm starts from the same initial state and uses the same batch-index
sequence. Training runs on the Colab free-tier GPU in resumable 1,000-update chunks (model,
optimiser, scheduler and RNG state); every outcome-bearing evaluation runs locally on CPU in
float64 from hashed weights. Training came in three waves: a smoke wave, a first wave (NA-s1,
NA-s2, ZC-s1) and a second wave (the other nine, released only after the learnability gate said
GO). The local stages prepared pools, registries, oracle and scoring checks and the training
bundles; ingested each wave with integrity checks, the learnability gate and learning curves;
and finally evaluated, decided and unsealed.

\textbf{Primary estimand.} Each eligible test history $h$ (a cue present, age $n\ge8$, end vertex
next to the anchor) is paired with an exact uniform draw $h'$ from its own Parikh class (same
prefix, cue, age, end vertex and directed crossing counts). With
$\Delta\hat q=\hat p_c(h)-\hat p_c(h')$ and $\Delta q^*=q^*_c(h)-q^*_c(h')$ on the cue-normalised laws,
\begin{equation}
\beta_{\mathrm{ord}}(m)=\frac{\sum_i\langle\Delta\hat q_i,\Delta q^*_i\rangle}{\sum_i\|\Delta q^*_i\|^2}.
\end{equation}
Every function of the Parikh key scores exactly 0 (the pair shares the key), every function of
the cue-free segment---including every function of the last 8 symbols---scores 0 in expectation,
and the oracle scores 1. The control-adjusted slope is
$\tilde\beta(s)=\beta_{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathrm{NA}\text{-}s)-\max\{\beta_{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathrm{init}(s)),
\overline{\beta}_{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathrm{AB}),\overline{\beta}_{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathrm{ZC})\}$, and
\textbf{C-ORD} is supported iff $\mathrm{mean}_s\tilde\beta(s)\ge0.25$, its 97.5\% bootstrap lower
bound exceeds 0.05, $\tilde\beta(s)>0$ in at least 3 of 4 seeds, and a tail diagnostic passes.

\textbf{Log-loss claims and the empirical-Bernstein bound.} For the quantised, $\varepsilon$-smoothed
readout ($Q=2^{20}$, $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$) the per-row excess loss
$X_i=\KL(q^*(H_i)\,\|\,p^Q(H_i))$ lies in $[0,B]$ with $B=17$. With $n=2m=2^{21}$ rows,
$\bar X=\tfrac1n\sum X_i$, $Z_k=(X_{2k-1}-X_{2k})^2/2$, $\bar Z=\tfrac1m\sum Z_k$, $L=\ln(2/\delta)$,
$a=B\sqrt{L/m}$, $b=B^2L/(12m)$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{up}}=(a+\sqrt{a^2+4(\bar Z+b)})/2$, the protocol
proves
\begin{equation}
P\Big(\mu\le\bar X+\sigma_{\mathrm{up}}\sqrt{2L/n}+\frac{BL}{3n}\Big)\ge1-\delta
\end{equation}
from a one-sided Bernstein moment bound and a paired U-statistic for the variance,
and computes the bound outward in exact and directed-rounding
arithmetic. \textbf{SHARP}(s) holds iff the bound at $\delta=0.025/4$ is below the stage-0 lower
endpoint of $G_{\mathrm{Parikh}}(32)$: then model NA-$s$ is not a function of the Parikh statistic or
any coarser one. A secondary TARGET requires recovering 25\% of that gap. Designed criteria use
the bag- and bigram-matched AB-versus-BA family (3,090 pairs).

\textbf{Learnability gate.} Before any order statistic exists, an NA pilot model must have natural
excess log-loss credibly below the exact window-8 gap $G_8(32)=8.855\times10^{-3}$ nats on fresh
validation rows (bootstrap upper bound at 97.5\%). Passing means ``better than every function of
the last 8 symbols''; it does not mean order use. GO iff NA-s1 or NA-s2 passes. Only statuses and
the decision are released; the statistics are sealed.

reclassification.

histories produced a weak but reproducible sensitivity to the order of seam crossings,
present in every seed and absent from the commutative controls, at about one sixteenth of the
oracle's order information. It falls far short of the order use the study was designed to
detect, and the designed probes do not show it. Whether more training, larger models or a
different curriculum would close the gap is a new question.

\subsection{Why does a commutative control respond to the designed placebo?}\label{sec:x6zc}
\textbf{Question.} The trained crossing-order study was INCONCLUSIVE only because one commutative
control (seed~1, trained on the commutative $\mathbb{Z}_3$ version of the process) responded to
the designed W2 contrast (placebo 0.209). The stored values showed two other controls responding
to the designed W1 contrast ($-0.133$ and $-0.218$). Why do they respond, and can the
designed-placebo machinery be reused?
\textbf{Design.} No training. An exact computation shows that on these designed families the
non-abelian oracle's answer for one ordering equals the commutative answer, and for the other
ordering equals the commutative answer with one crossing missed. Any order-dependent miscount by
a commutatively trained model therefore shows up along the order contrast, although an exactly
commutative predictor gives zero. The registered test reverses the direction of one or both
loops on never-used histories. The order contrast stays the same in every variant (ratio~1),
while every sign-symmetric miscount reverses sign when both loops are reversed (ratio~$-1$).
The 16 stored designed results were first reproduced bit for bit.
\textbf{What ran.} Three stages on 26--27 October, all gates passed, 3.9 CPU-hours in all.
\textbf{Result} (\fpath{x6zc-store/report.json}; 1,024 fresh histories). The seed-1 control's
W2 response replicated (0.207, interval 0.205--0.209). Relative to it, the response is 0.06 with
both loops reversed, and 0.18 and 0.83 with one loop reversed. It is not invariant, as order use
would be, and it does not reverse, as a symmetric miscount would. The registered outcome is
UNEXPLAINED (not invariant to loop reversal). The two W1 controls' responses also replicated and
reversed sign when both loops were reversed, so they are not order use in the oracle's
direction. They fit an order-dependent miscount or an order-free effect of token identity, which
W1 cannot separate. Fresh controls were stable, and removing matched or coincident pairs changed
no outcome.
\textbf{Consequences.} The designed-placebo comparison may be reused as a behavioural contrast
only with explicit empirical controls; it must not be described as automatically excluding every
predictor trained on a commutative process. The crossing-order verdict stays INCONCLUSIVE.

Each takeover wrote an assumption record, because release verifiers bind the issuer's identity;
one takeover race was resolved by having the late session stand down. Workers were headless
sessions or subagents launched per job, with context caps that forced rotation to fresh
sessions; a transfer record registers each new worker identity. More than 100 jobs had been
created by 26 October. Two
operational hazards recurred: a shared subscription hitting usage limits (handled by waiting,
never by switching accounts), and permission classifiers blocking some actions (never
bypassed). I approved and installed an automatic-resume scheduler.

\subsection{The cloud training pipeline and its integrity gates}
The trained crossing-order study was the first trained on a GPU. The coordinator builds deterministic bundles (code
archive, data, initial states, a ticket with seeds, steps and checkpoint interval, expected batch
and RNG digests for every chunk) and releases a bundle hash to me, and I run a fixed
notebook in the browser; only one cell (the bundle hash) may be edited. Every chunk records its
batch digest and PCG64 state digest; the export carries a manifest whose hash I relay.
Locally, four checks run per model before any evaluation: a weights check (manifest, inventory
and canonical weights digests), a provenance check (every session and execution record matches
the permitted cell templates, commit, protocol and bundle), a data-identity check (data, initial
state, per-chunk batch and RNG digests), and a completeness check (a linked chain of exactly one
record per checkpoint). An
admission lock then fixes the model's only admissible chain. GPU training is not claimed to be
reproducible; data, batch sequence, initial state and every evaluation are.

\subsection{Defects found and corrected}
The correction route was exercised many times, always before any outcome-bearing quantity existed
or with documented independence from it. Examples: a $2\pi$ phase edge in the first four-symbol
origin attempt; a module split under the 400-line rule in the orientability oracle study; an
integer/string key mismatch and the ridge tolerance (discussed above) in counterfactual forcing;

classes of defect were caught only by real runs: those where tests exercised synthetic inputs
but not the real output of a reused function (the \texttt{Fraction(dict)} case), and those that
depend on the live environment (the pasted cell). Both led to added checks.

\section{Threats to validity and limitations}\label{sec:threats}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{One scoped causal claim.} The pilot evidence is representational, and the first two
intervention designs stopped at gates. The one causal-use result concerns a single registered
linear code, at one position and site, for one kind of counterfactual (an $x$-history rotation
with the last symbol kept); it excludes neither other encodings nor other carriers. Its necessity
follow-up is tied to it by an exact identity, and its decoy test covers only constructed decoys.
\item \textbf{One toy setting.} One small architecture and recipe, synthetic HMMs on tiny lattices,
a few seeds (three in the pilot, at most four per arm). Nothing is claimed about natural language
or large models, other architectures, or populations of seeds.
\item \textbf{Statistics.} Pilot decisions use sign consistency and a data-derived envelope, not
significance tests; panels are partitions within runs. Most later bounds are nominal bootstrap
bounds; only the log-loss claims of the trained crossing-order study carry a finite-sample
guarantee.
\item \textbf{Measurement choices.} Vietoris--Rips over $\mathbb{F}_2$, $M=512$, the longest bar as
$S$; four one-factor sensitivity variants. Circular coordinates use one cocycle and can give
disconnected points arbitrary offsets.
\item \textbf{Exposure.} The circular audit, the counterfactual-forcing study, the four-symbol
origin test, the readout-attribution study and its necessity and decoy follow-up reuse the
three pilot models, whose test panels
had been unblinded; they draw fresh histories protected by registries, but the models are not
fresh.
\item \textbf{Margins and controls.} The four-symbol margin sits above the oracle's own value; the
P control is one-sided; the learning-speed kernel is confounded with speed.
\item \textbf{Reproducibility scope.} One machine and environment; one of 17 pilot trainings was
retrained; the clean-rerun gate formally failed; GPU training in the crossing-order study is not
reproducible by design.
\item \textbf{Process.} One post-data tolerance change (in the counterfactual-forcing study)
occurred without my prior approval and I
ratified it afterwards. Records written by agents were reviewed by other agents; I approved decision
points but did not audit every computation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Under controlled conditions, next-token training on a hidden walk produces a learned circle that
reflects the world's topology, aligns with Bayesian belief geometry, and exceeds what the last
symbol permits. Where it comes from is only partly known: the last-symbol embedding carries much of
it, beliefs converge after a few symbols, and the beyond-four-symbol covariance of the learned phase
is below a margin that even the oracle's phase does not reach. The model does use it: at the
final readout, the registered circle code carries about 85\% of the model's response to a change
in the $x$-history, in all three models; it is the main carrier but not the only one, and on
decoys whose readout ignores the code the same method says ``not used''. The follow-up programme mostly produced well-defined stops rather than findings, and the
stops taught something about one-sided controls, nonlinear encodings, non-specific interventions,
fragile chart rules and margins set without oracle checks. The trained crossing-order study was
designed to avoid each of these failure modes on a process where order provably matters. It
reached its decision with every validity gate passed and found weak but reproducible natural
order sensitivity in every seed, far below the registered effect size, so its preregistered
verdict is INCONCLUSIVE and not a claim of order use. The readout-attribution study answered
the use question with an exact, linear attribution at the model's readout, after the two
earlier intervention designs had failed for reasons of method. For the origin question, an oracle
sweep found a nearby world whose beyond-four signal is 4.7 times the registered margin. A
training pilot there will show whether the question can be asked again with room to answer it.
