# X5: geometry, information distribution and learnability

13 October 2026; snapshot `f8afc2a5e43309aa4bf2d402f2be4e38470df180`. **Planning only; no generation, fitting, training or experiments performed.** [R/M/L/H/S] have the meanings in [Research Landscape](RESEARCH_LANDSCAPE.md). All thresholds here are proposed design choices requiring a new protocol; original preregistrations remain unchanged.

## Revision after E4 and scientific scope

E4 supplies robust O/P representation results and strengthens R1/F1 competitors; it supplies no curvature intervention or learning-time calibration. Preserve G0/G1/G2 and the construction below. Initial G1 is **a comparison of two specified metric-coupled stochastic processes**, not an isolated causal effect of curvature. Geometry, process difficulty and representation geometry are different objects. The≥250-step rule is retained as a prospective practical threshold; no existing calibration validates it as a universal effect scale.

## Question and variables

**[H5, primary training hypothesis]** For one prespecified cylinder process, a metric-dependent anisotropic transition kernel increases steps to an oracle-relative regret threshold by at least 250 steps compared with the flat-kernel reference, under the same topology, stationary occupancy and emissions. This is deliberately a hypothesis about **two defined stochastic processes**, not a general claim that curvature impedes learning.

Competing explanations include faster learning, unchanged learning, altered Bayesian uncertainty, changed spectral mixing, changed context gain, weaker statistical coverage and optimizer sensitivity. Matching topology does not match any of those quantities automatically.

| Variable | Mathematical meaning | Primary G1 plan |
|---|---|---|
| Topology | Underlying filled cylinder complex | Fixed vertex/cell incidence and periodic gluing |
| Metric/curvature | Riemannian reference and its finite discretization | Manipulated by f(y) |
| Dynamics T | Law of a next hidden state | Changes by a fixed metric-to-conductance rule |
| Emissions E | Symbol law given hidden state | Same numeric E as baseline, not rebuilt from new metric |
| Occupancy μ | Stationary law and path coverage | Stationary law fixed uniform; path autocorrelation may change |

Metric alone cannot enter a token-only learner unless it changes the observation law. Keep generator geometry separate from geometric quantities inferred from the learner's representations.

## Mathematical world and generation contracts

### Continuum reference

**[M]** Let `M=S1×[0,11]`, θ∈[0,2π), with

\[
g_f=dy^2+f(y)^2d\theta^2,\qquad K(y)=-f''(y)/f(y),\qquad d\mathrm{vol}=f(y)d\theta dy.
\]

Set R=12/(2π) so a flat circumference has length 12, matching the pilot's x-periodic axis. Compare

\[
f_0(y)=R,\qquad f_a(y)=R[1+a\cos(2\pi y/11)],\quad a=0.20.
\]

Both are positive and have the same total area `2π∫fdy=132`; topology and area agree, pointwise circumference and curvature do not. The curvature formula follows from the warped-product metric; sign and magnitude vary with y. It is not an extrinsic bending of a flat cylinder. Isometrically bending a flat sheet changes its appearance without changing intrinsic curvature.

### Discrete process

Reuse the 12×12 cylinder complex, θx=2πx/12, y=0,...,11, N=144. Horizontal edge lengths are `lf(y)=f(y)·2π/12`; vertical lengths are 1. Wrapped horizontal edges follow the same rule. Distances `df` are weighted shortest-path distances on this fixed graph, or a separately declared numerical geodesic approximation. **Weighted graph distances are a discrete metric proxy, not an exact realization of the continuum metric or a curvature estimator.** Do not label a finite difference of arbitrary graph distances Gaussian curvature.

For G1 use symmetric edge conductances `cij=1/lij²`, zero off edges. Let `Ca=max_i Σ_j cij`; define

\[
T_{f,ij}=0.8c_{ij}/C_a\ (i\ne j),\qquad
T_{f,ii}=1-0.8\sum_j c_{ij}/C_a.
\]

This is row-stochastic, symmetric, lazy with self-loop ≥0.2, edge-supported, and has π=1/144. At a=0 it exactly recovers the core max-degree walk. Verify these identities and state ordering before any sampling. Record Ca and spectra; the normalization changes average jump rate as well as local anisotropy. This is an explicit confound of interpreting an effect as “curvature.” A secondary globally time-rescaled flat walk can match mean jump rate; matching a single rate does not match the full spectrum.

This process is **not** Brownian motion generated by the Laplace–Beltrami operator: its invariant measure is uniform over vertices, not proportional to f(y). Choosing area-stationary diffusion would couple curvature, dynamics and occupancy and require a different plan. No hidden coordinate or conductance is supplied to the learner.

Store the original O emission array, σ=1.25, 36 symbols, identical state order and symbol permutation in both G1 arms. The emission field is deliberately not smooth under the same metric in both arms; that is how E is held fixed. Retain original 33-observation/32-context alignment and public-token training.

## Information organization: use existing O/P first

**[R]** O/P keep the same row multiset, πE, state-conditional emission entropy and axis factorization, while changing spatial organization relative to T. Nevertheless context gain and future geometry differ. Under P, the model's higher regret and missing H1 cannot be interpreted as lost topology alone. Existing E3 trajectories/checkpoints already permit an exploratory comparison of oracle-relative learning curves, alignment and variability; no new O/P training is necessary for that question.

| Emission organization | Precise candidate | What it changes and gate |
|---|---|---|
| Smooth local | Existing O | Reference; match its oracle and short-context baselines |
| Axis-permuted | Existing P | Preserves emission rows and factors; changes temporal organization and observability |
| Multimodal | Mixture of two separated center kernels per symbol, fixed pairs | New E; test whether distinct states become predictively aliased before training |
| Hierarchical | Fixed coarse-region/within-region symbol factorization | New E, possibly alphabet semantics/size; keep alphabet fixed where possible and report all changed quantities |
| Irregular | Existing full-state permutation of O, if supported by the selected entry point | Preserves row multiset under uniform π, can destroy factorization; new training if compared prospectively |

Do not combine these five organizations with two metrics and multiple densities immediately. Multimodal/hierarchical emissions only merit training if the oracle distinguishes a question not answered by O/P.

## Density and coverage are separate experiments

Three meanings of density must be kept distinct: stationary state occupancy, distribution of histories in training, and density of activation points chosen for PH.

**[H5-S, analysis-only question]** At fixed checkpoint and token-generating process, deliberately reducing coverage around a cycle can remove a PH signature while the original full-evaluation-set oracle-relative regret stays fixed by construction. Subsample aligned stored history clouds by a predeclared public/oracle selection rule. Compute identical selection in belief/future and activation spaces. This manipulation changes the estimator's sample, not what the model learned. The selected subpopulation's average regret may differ and must be reported separately. Compare random thinning at the same M; use M=256/512 and record angular coverage, entropy and effective sample size. Any selected sample requires recalibration of PH and cannot replace primary F1.

Stationary occupancy manipulation generally requires changing T. The current TransitionSpec requires symmetry, so a connected irreducible T has uniform stationary law. A Metropolis or area-stationary kernel with nonuniform μ cannot be forced into that contract. It requires an explicit generalized-kernel contract with reversibility/stationarity checks, versioning and review (**D**), while oracle numerical kernels can often remain unchanged.

An independent initial-law comparison is possible with T/E fixed: `μ0,a(z)∝exp(a cos θz)`, a=1 versus uniform, then sample the same 33-step walks. This changes **transient** occupancy and observation law, not the stationary distribution. Store π as the stationary law and μ0 as a separate initial-law field; current dataset stages use π, so a small sampler/oracle adapter and metadata are needed (D). The oracle must start with μ0 for each condition. Evaluation under uniform μ0 is a distribution shift whose conditional predictions can differ because beliefs depend on the prior. It is not pure loss of training coverage.

A low-impact alternative is evaluation stratification only: rare histories by frozen oracle log-evidence, boundaries by posterior boundary mass, and poorly covered cycle arcs by posterior angle with sufficiently large resultant. Thresholds are fit/validation-defined and fixed before test. Hidden states may label privileged diagnostics, never learner inputs. Repeated positions from one trajectory are not independent units.

## Minimal programme and decision rules

### Stage 0: reuse and identifiability, A/B

One main question: does the proposed process still provide enough history-dependent information to make a learning-time comparison meaningful? Existing O/P curves are exploratory. For the G1 pair, calculate on calibration histories only: H(W|Z), H(W), Bayes conditional entropy by position, context gain GC, short-context oracle regret, observability spectra, transition spectral gap, mean move probability and world/emission/belief/future PH. Requirements: kernel checks pass, every symbol probability≥1e−4, and GC≥0.03 nats in both arms. Otherwise stop with a weak-context result; do not retune a after seeing test performance.

### Required movement-rate and mixing comparison before curvature interpretation

For both kernels report r=Σ_z π_z(1−T_zz), eigenvalues/gap, autocorrelation/integrated correlation of fixed state/emission diagnostics, and a mixing proxy or bound with assumptions. Uniform stationary occupancy does not match any of these. Construct, before training, T_flat,α=I+α(T_flat−I), α=r_curved/r_flat, only if nonnegative/lazy invariants remain valid. Report whether this matches rate and how much its gap/correlation differ. This third kernel is initially an **exact-process diagnostic, not a new training arm**.

If rate/gap/mixing mismatch remains, curvature-specific attribution is blocked. Even matching these summaries leaves anisotropy, entropy/context gain and full transition correlations as alternatives; a later matched comparison must specify which are controlled. Do not imply an impossible scalar α matches an entire spectrum. A process-level G1 comparison remains worthwhile if both oracle/context gates pass. If a later speed-matched training pair explains the effect, drop curvature specificity; if it does not, retain the residual alternatives rather than declaring curvature identified.

### Stage 1: one paired G1 experiment, C/D

Six **new prospective** models: two kernels ×3 paired master seeds; O only; one architecture; 5000 steps; batch128; 131072 training trajectories per arm. Share initial state sampling/emission uniform streams where valid, model initial weights, symbol naming and batch-index streams within a pair. Different T still produces different training histories; pairing reduces nuisance variance rather than creating identical data. Preserve paired seeds in the manifest independently of arm-specific artifact IDs. Existing flat-O models are exploratory anchors, not substitutes for fresh paired controls after protocol selection.

Primary outcome τ: first evaluation checkpoint at which `regret/GC≤0.05` on the fixed dev set, sustained at the next two evaluations. Proposed grid every 100 steps to 1000, then every 250 to 5000, with a final confirmation at 5000 for crossings close to the end; if the last two confirmations are unavailable the crossing is censored. GC is estimated on a separately frozen calibration set per arm. Report the interval between the prior grid point and first crossing. Never interpolate the original 12-checkpoint E3 curves into precise hitting times.

The≥250-step threshold is an unchanged proposed minimum useful delay, not calibrated by E3/E4 timings or fitted after test. Denser evaluation only changes resolution, not the success criterion.

**Decision:** support H5 for this three-pair cohort if every uncensored pair has `τcurved−τflat≥250` steps and the difference remains positive under paired dev-trajectory resampling. Reject the all-three practical-effect claim if any pair's upper uncertainty bound is below 250; otherwise inconclusive. Right-censored times can imply a lower bound if sufficient, but never substitute 5000 as the observed time. Report each pair and threshold-failure rate. Three seed pairs do not supply strong population-level power; use a later independently approved replication to generalize.

Primary learning outcome is conditional on the process-specific oracle. Secondary outcomes: final fresh-test regret (nats), normalized regret, area under regret/GC curve, failure to meet threshold, per-seed variation, rare-history regret and GC-stratified regret. Small GC makes normalization unstable, hence the gate. Lower raw CE may merely reflect lower Bayes entropy. Equal normalized thresholds still do not prove equal statistical difficulty.

Training steps count optimizer updates; exposure is `steps×128×33` target tokens, about21.1M at 5000. Distinguish repeated exposure from the number of unique sampled training histories. Add a small independent **data-volume** pair only if needed: keeping compute fixed while changing unique trajectories estimates a statistical-data effect, not curvature alone.

### Stage 2: a single follow-up, conditional

If G1 has a reproducible learning-time effect, compare one flat global-speed-matched kernel against the curved kernel to challenge the mixing-time explanation. If that explains the effect, drop a curvature-specific claim. If G1 does not produce a meaningful effect, do not launch G2×density×architecture factorials to search for one.

Alternatively select G2 only if an oracle gate shows that emission organization tests a distinct mechanism. Select density training only after analysis-only thinning demonstrates adequate estimator reliability. Each follow-up has its own main hypothesis, fresh evaluation set and budget; neither is automatically included in H5.

## Optimization diagnostics and negative outcomes

**[M/L]** Bayesian uncertainty is part of the data law; sample complexity concerns finite data; optimization concerns progress of a specified algorithm; representation complexity is a separate measurement. [Kiani, Wang and Weber](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01461) do not imply that larger curvature causes slower gradient descent here, and [Rahaman et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/rahaman19a.html) motivate competing frequency mechanisms rather than a monotone law.

If a model fails the threshold, first report failure and rule out numerical/sampling issues. A failure is not proof of local minima. Gradient norms, clip frequency and validation oscillation can diagnose dynamics if logged prospectively. Estimating Hessian condition numbers or loss barriers is additional work with uncertain interpretation; defer it unless learning curves motivate a specific test. Any optimizer change is a separately declared rescue arm, not deletion of a failed seed.

Measure S and oracle/model geometry at a small fixed subset `{0,100,500,1000,5000}`, using all 8 panels only at 0/5000. Correlation of topology emergence with loss is observational. Distinguish raw S, world contrasts, and rare-region coverage. Avoid identifying a “topological learning phase” by selecting the checkpoint or PH summary after inspecting curves.

Positive: the defined coupled process slows the specified algorithm. Negative: it fails the stated ≥250-step all-three effect or speeds learning; no monotone curvature inference. Inconclusive: context/observability gate fails, crossings are unresolved, variance dominates, a model crashes, or PH calibration cannot distinguish density artifacts from representation change.

## Shared harness mapping and interpretation boundary

A is the same cylinder complex plus separate metric metadata; B is the weighted kernel with fixed numeric E, uniform π and μ0; C reuses exact HMM tasks/oracles; D reuses the current Transformer; E defines geometry and the process contrast; F calibrates PH/learning endpoints; G declares metadata-only G0, T-changing G1 and separate E-changing G2; H reports typed regret/τ/geometry with pairing/censoring/provenance. See [architecture CaseC](RESEARCH_HARNESS_ARCHITECTURE.md#c--worked-future-stress-test-x5-g1).

X5 G1 is the proposed **first architectural stress test**, after X4 and selection of one scientific follow-up, because it reuses the existing oracle semantics and changes a familiar process input. This does not authorize or require it. Multimodal/hierarchical/density alternatives remain deferred.

Positive≥250-step results justify slower learning by the specified algorithm on this metric-coupled process pair, conditional on the oracle gates and cohort. They do not justify a monotone curvature law, local minima, topology-caused learning speed or generalization to other worlds/architectures. A null effect is a useful rejection of this practical process comparison; a failed oracle/calibration gate is inconclusive for optimization. Sampling changes and a shift in initial prior need their own target oracle, not a reused stationary prior.
